# Traffic response to fuel price (elasticity)

**Status:** figures copied from a research report the project owner pasted
into the chat on 17 Nov 2026. That report cited each paper. **We have not
opened the papers.** Check each figure against its source before quoting it
outside this repo.

**Elasticity** means the percentage change in traffic for a 1% change in
price. An elasticity of -0.1 means a 10% price rise cuts traffic by 1%.

## Reported estimates

| Source (as cited) | Finding | Elasticity |
|---|---|---|
| Goodwin, Dargay and Hanly (2004), review | Lasting 10% rise cuts traffic about 1% within a year, 3% over five years | -0.1 short run, -0.29 long run |
| Wardman (2022), review of 204 British studies | Overall car demand; kilometres driven | -0.15 short run; about -0.18 for kilometres |
| HM Treasury and HMRC (2014), used by the OBR | Fuel demand | -0.07 short run, -0.13 medium run |
| Switzerland, Germany, Austria (2025), monthly traffic counts | Smaller than the UK figures | -0.086 |
| Knittel and Tanaka (2019), Japan | Fuel use falls more for a rise than it grows for a fall | -0.42 for a rise, -0.22 for a fall |

The Japan result is one study from another country. It only hints that UK
drivers may respond unevenly.

## Reported on breakdowns

- No published study ties UK fuel prices to roadside-assistance demand.
- The AA said high prices and the cost-of-living crisis were thought to have
  added to its 10,500 out-of-fuel and out-of-charge call-outs in 2022. It has
  also said it saw no rise in out-of-fuel breakdowns during another price surge.
- Two 2022 RAC survey results: 41% of drivers said they were already driving
  less, and some were putting off servicing and repairs. That could raise
  breakdowns per mile, with a delay of months.
- Suggested test: use your own call-out reason codes (stage 2).
